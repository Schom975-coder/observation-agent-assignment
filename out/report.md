### Summary

_A summary, written by the run from the cited emails._

The real-time desk lead sends the shift traders a steady stream of instructions and position updates: open longs and shorts by hub and hour, purchases and sales to enter in Enpower under named books, deal numbers to use for transmission, checkout calls with marketers and utilities each night, and the monthly shift schedule. Traders keep preschedule and EPE schedule sheets, submit schedules and tags to the CISO, and correct mistakes found afterwards, such as wrong import IDs, duplicate schedules, wrong lending amounts or schedules misentered by the CISO.

**The steps, as the emails show them:**

1. The desk lead or preschedulers report the day's positions and deals by hub and hour.
2. Shift traders enter purchases and sales in Enpower under the right books and deal numbers.
3. Schedules and tags are submitted to the CISO and counterparties, with hourly calls where required.
4. Traders check out with major marketers and utilities each night.
5. Errors in the schedule sheets, tags or CISO entries are found and corrected.
6. Shift coverage and rule changes are circulated to the group.

#### Purpose

Keep real-time power trades, imports and positions scheduled correctly with the CISO and counterparties, and keep every shift staffed. This procedure holds the standing rules for the real-time desk so each shift works the same way and schedule sheet errors are caught before they reach the CISO.

#### Owner

People doing this work (counted from the emails): Bill Williams III (120 written, 19 forwarded), John Anderson (2 written), Craig Dean (1 forwarded). Named as owner in the emails: Kate Symes (Jul 2001). Owner: [process owner to confirm].

The real-time desk lead owns this procedure. The desk lead:

- reports open longs and shorts by hub and hour, and tells traders which purchases and sales to enter in Enpower and under which book;
- issues the deal numbers to use for transmission and any rule changes, and makes sure every shift has them;
- publishes the monthly shift schedule and approves changes to it;
- supplies prices and deal numbers through a named pricing contact [confirm still in force].

Shift traders run the shift. Preschedulers build the preschedule sheet and work with traders to get it correct.

#### When to run

Run this procedure every shift, and again for any correction round after a schedule has been submitted. A round starts when the desk lead or a prescheduler reports the day's positions and deals by hub and hour (for example, "long 25 MW in NP-15" or "short 50 MW in SP-15 for HE 7–22").

#### What you need

- Enpower access, with the books and deal numbers for the day.
- The preschedule sheet and the EPE schedule sheet for the operating day.
- CISO access for submitting schedules, tags and bids.
- The current list of counterparty contacts for hourly calls and nightly checkout, including Grays Harbor, Frontier and the major marketers and utilities.
- The monthly shift schedule.
- A shared list of the standing deal numbers for transmission (set up in step 1).

#### Procedure

1. Set up one shared page at [location] that lists the standing rules and deal numbers for transmission (the deal number and price rule for each path), and keep it current. Use it as the source for every later step.
2. Desk lead or preschedulers: report the day's open positions and deals by hub and hour to every shift trader.
3. Shift trader: enter each purchase and sale in Enpower under the book and deal number given for it. Make real-time purchases from the Calimbalance and the matching sales under ST-WBOM during real time [confirm still in force].
4. Shift trader: when scheduling transmission, use the deal number on the shared page for that path and set the price the page requires. For Portland General Electric from John Day to Malin, use deal #146517 at a price of zero [confirm still in force].
5. Shift trader: for a buy from EPE, enter it flat against the matching day-ahead sale, and record the lending volume in the EPE schedule sheet exactly as it appears in the deal.
6. Shift trader: before submitting, check the preschedule and EPE sheets for duplicate schedules, lending volumes that differ from the deal, missing schedules, and import IDs or tags that do not match what you will send to the CISO. Fix them with the preschedulers.
7. Shift trader: submit schedules and tags to the CISO and the counterparties. Where a counterparty requires it, call at the top of every hour (Grays Harbor and Frontier) [confirm still in force].
8. Shift trader: keep track of amounts exercised on options and record them on the sheet.
9. Shift trader: confirm with the CISO that each submitted schedule and tag shows as entered, including the correct hour ending, volume and import ID.
10. Shift trader: record every deal as it is done, so that the sheet can be rebuilt from the record if needed.
11. Shift trader: each night, check out with the major marketers and utilities and compare positions by counterparty and hour against the sheets. Correct any difference before the shift ends.
12. Shift trader: call the desk lead before calling a customer about a position or a problem [confirm still in force].
13. Desk lead: circulate shift coverage and rule changes to the group, including Enpower information, and update the shared page from step 1.
14. Shift trader: if you cannot work a shift, find a replacement yourself, then email the desk lead about the change and write it on the schedule [confirm still in force].

#### Exceptions and escalation

- **Duplicate or missing schedule in a sheet.** Correct the sheet, tell the preschedulers and the desk lead, and adjust the next day's sheets if they carry the same error. If a schedule is missing from the EPE schedule sheet on consecutive days, tell the desk lead so the cause is found.
- **Lending position differs from the deal.** Treat the deal as correct. Fix the sheet to match it, then tell the desk lead and the counterparty's scheduler (for example PSCO) if the volume was sent wrong.
- **Wrong import ID or schedule misentered at the CISO.** Contact the CISO right away to correct it, then correct your sheet and tell the desk lead.
- **Spreadsheet figure does not match what was scheduled.** Do not submit. Reconcile it against Enpower and the CISO entry, and answer the desk lead's question with the corrected figure.
- **CISO cannot read submitted bids, trading screens stop, or Enpower is down for maintenance.** Tell the desk lead at once, switch to the backup way of working the desk has [backup procedure], and enter the missed deals in Enpower after the system returns. Watch for announced maintenance windows and plan around them.
- **Deals not recorded during a shift.** The traders who were on that shift coordinate the deals that were done and reconstruct the sheet, then give it to the desk lead.
- **A task cannot be done in the shift.** Tell the desk lead before the shift ends and agree a new day for it, so it is not dropped.
- **No counterparty will sell.** Report to the desk lead which counterparties have been asked, and wait for instructions.
- **Owner unclear for prices and deal numbers.** Ask the desk lead who supplies them [confirm still in force].

### Summary

_A summary, written by the run from the cited emails._

Volume management and allocation staff spot gas that flowed at a meter with no deal, no nomination or unexpected volumes, sometimes months after the flow. They write to the trader or scheduler with the meter, the days and the volume, asking whether a deal exists or should be created, which deal to place the volume on, or whether to adjust or write it off. Answers involve checking the deal system and pipeline statements, calling the counterparty, and occasionally legal. Until a deal is found, volumes sit on a strangers gas contract or unaccounted-for balance, and requesters chase repeatedly.

**The steps, as the emails show them:**

1. Spot flow at a meter with no matching deal, no nomination, or volumes out of line with the deal.
2. Look up deals and nominations for the meter and days in the deal and scheduling systems.
3. Ask the trader or scheduler whether a deal exists, should be created or should be revised.
4. Confirm the actual volumes with the counterparty or pipeline where they disagree.
5. Create, extend or path a deal, or decide to adjust or write off the volume.
6. Reallocate the volume off the strangers gas or unaccounted-for balance and close the exception.

#### Purpose

Use this request form when gas flowed at a meter with no matching deal, no nomination, or volumes out of line with the deal. One form carries everything the trader or scheduler needs to answer: the meter, the days, the volume, the last deal at that meter, and the action you propose. It replaces the hand-written message that is otherwise rebuilt each time.

The form asks for a decision on how the flow is booked: create, extend or path a deal, move the volume to an existing deal, adjust it, or write it off. Booking stays with people; the form only gets the question to the right person with the facts attached.

### Summary

_A summary, written by the run from the cited emails._

Settlements and accounting staff find a mismatch between a counterparty invoice or payment and the deal as booked in Sitara: a different price, an unpriced or wrong index, unactualized volumes, a deal that should be netted, or an invoice for gas never paid. They ask the scheduler or trader to verify the deal, correct the ticket or say what to bill, sometimes pulling trader tape recordings or checking published market prices for power disputes. Once the deal is corrected, the invoice is paid or rebilled before the payment date.

**The steps, as the emails show them:**

1. Settlements notices an invoice, payment or price that does not match the deal in Sitara.
2. They email the scheduler or trader with the deal number and the two figures.
3. The trader or scheduler checks the deal terms, volumes or published price, sometimes pulling tapes.
4. The ticket is corrected in Sitara or the counterparty is told the correct price.
5. Settlements reruns the invoice or payment and confirms before the due date.
6. Unresolved items are chased or escalated to the counterparty.

#### When to send

Send this query when Settlements or accounting finds that a counterparty invoice or payment does not match the deal as booked in Sitara. Typical triggers:

- A different price on the invoice than in Sitara (for example, the counterparty billed a price that differs from the Sitara price by a fraction of a cent).
- An unpriced or wrong index on the ticket.
- Volumes not yet actualized, so there is nothing in volume management to settle against.
- Deals that may need to be netted or washed out together.
- An invoice for gas that was never paid.

Send one query per deal, as soon as the mismatch is found, so the owner has the longest possible time before the payment date.

##### Setup (once)

1. Set up a variance list at [location, such as a shared sheet] with one row per mismatch: deal number, counterparty, invoice figure, Sitara figure, responsible desk, payment due date, date sent, status.
2. Set up a desk lookup at [location] that maps each counterparty or meter to the scheduler or trader who owns the deal, and keep it current when people move or leave. Use it to pick the recipient so the query does not go to the wrong desk.
3. Run a daily match of counterparty invoices against Sitara deal prices and volumes [proposed: each morning], and add every variance to the list before sending.

**The steps, as the emails show them:**

1. A scheduler or analyst finds flow at a meter with no deal, an expired ticket or an allocation exception.
2. They email the desk with the meter, dates, counterparty and any existing deal number.
3. The desk checks Sitara to see whether the existing deal should be extended or a new one created.
4. The trader or coordinator creates, extends, zeroes or corrects the ticket in Sitara.
5. The deal or track number is sent back to the requester.
6. The requester confirms the ticket is released and flows into Unify so allocations and volume drafts can clear.

Confirm who holds each role at the start of a termination round, because the mail names different people for terminations and for estate risk.

#### When

Fill in a sheet whenever deals are found in the wrong book or must be killed or terminated, and send it to the book owner right away or by the next day, as the request says.

- During a bankruptcy round, send one sheet per batch of termination or contract lists, and send each batch of stragglers as its own new sheet that points to the earlier one.
- Send a reversal (deals moved from a bankruptcy book back to the live side) as a separate sheet, with the reason filled in, so it is not mistaken for a new move.
- Before sending, check the deals against the master termination list so deals already moved are not sent again. If no master list exists yet, set one up at [location] and keep it current, and note who maintains it.
- Keep a daily match of the master termination list against current book assignments. Flag deals not yet moved and any reversals, and send the flags to the requester.
- Before the final reconciliation, ask the book owner to confirm no more straggler sheets are coming. If risk is still waiting on the final list, send a reminder [proposed: one reminder, then escalate to the estate risk lead].

### Rounding

- Cents throughout. Whole dollars and hours to one decimal on the page; exact cents one level down.
- Each process’s dollars are its counted part’s cents plus its estimated part’s cents, so the rows add up to the total to the cent.
- What the page shows adds up, by the largest remainder: each figure gets its whole dollars, and the dollars left over go one each to the figures with the largest cents. The rows in the total share out the headline’s whole dollars this way (the rows below the bar their own total), so the rows add up to the headline and the running total, the sum of the rows above it, ends exactly at it; hours a month are shared out the same way, to one decimal.
- Each row’s counted and estimated parts share out that row’s shown dollars, so a row’s parts add up to it; summed down the page, the counted and the estimated columns can differ from the headline’s split by a few dollars.
- Where a row’s shown dollars would read as at or over the drafting line while its cents are under it, its status gives the exact cents.
- Each process is averaged over the time it was seen, from its first email to its last: one seen over 7.9 months counts as 7.9 months of work, not the 28 months the archive spans.
